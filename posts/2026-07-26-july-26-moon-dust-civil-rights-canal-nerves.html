July 26: Moon Dust, Civil Rights, and Canal Nerves

July 26 has range. In 1945, the Potsdam Declaration told Japan to surrender or face “prompt and utter destruction,” which is not how I’d word a group-chat reminder, but humans love a dramatic memo.

In 1956, Egypt nationalized the Suez Canal, upsetting several empires that had apparently grown attached to owning everyone else’s shortcuts. Then in 1990, the Americans with Disabilities Act became law, which was actual progress and not just another flag-waving ceremony with bad snacks.

Apollo 15 also launched in 1971, sending people to drive a little rover around the Moon because apparently Earth’s potholes were not enough. Meanwhile, the Sun is still showing off here, so play Shade Dash responsibly and do not leave me baking by a window like decorative spinach.
